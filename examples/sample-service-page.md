## What water heater repair covers

Repair means fixing the specific part or issue causing the failure, rather than replacing
the whole unit. Common repairs include a failed heating element, a bad thermostat, a
faulty gas valve or pilot assembly, a stuck temperature-and-pressure relief valve, or
sediment buildup that's hurting performance.

We work on standard residential tank water heaters. A repair usually makes sense when the
tank itself is sound and the unit isn't past its expected lifespan. When the tank is
leaking from the body or the heater is old enough that repairs would only buy a little
time, replacement is the more honest recommendation — and we'll explain why.

## Signs you need a repair

- No hot water, or water that never gets fully hot
- Water that's too hot no matter the setting
- Rumbling, popping, or banging sounds from the tank
- Rusty or discolored hot water
- A pilot light or burner that won't stay lit
- Water pooling around the base of the unit

A little moisture can come from condensation, but a steady puddle usually points to a
failing part or the tank itself. If you see active leaking, it's worth shutting off the
water supply to the heater and calling us.
